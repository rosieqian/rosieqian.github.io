PORTFOLIO PROJECT 01 · GRC

Security Audit: Controls & Compliance

A Coursera portfolio activity completed as part of the Google Cybersecurity Professional Certificate.

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Purpose and approach.

I evaluated a fictional organization’s security posture by reviewing the audit scope, assets, current controls, and compliance considerations. The exercise required translating identified gaps into prioritized, practical recommendations.

This project demonstrates my ability to follow a defined audit process, connect controls to organizational risk, and communicate findings clearly for nontechnical stakeholders.

KEY FINDINGS

Priority gaps.

Access control

Limit access to sensitive information, formalize user-access policies, and apply least privilege so employees receive only the permissions required for their roles.

Data protection

Encrypt customer and payment-card data and strengthen how sensitive information is stored, processed, and transmitted.

Detection & recovery

Add intrusion detection, maintain reliable backups, and establish a disaster-recovery plan to improve visibility and resilience.

Asset management

Maintain a clearer asset inventory and regularly update, maintain, or replace legacy systems that increase exposure.

COMPLIANCE LENS

Standards considered.

PCI DSS: payment-card access, secure processing environments, encryption, and password management.

GDPR: protection of E.U. customer data, breach-notification planning, data inventory, and documented privacy practices.

SOC: access policies, confidentiality, data integrity, and availability.

RECOMMENDATION

Risk-based next steps.

Prioritize least privilege, encryption, stronger password controls, intrusion detection, backups, and disaster recovery. Follow with stronger asset tracking and a plan to update or replace older systems. Together, these improvements reduce exposure while moving the organization toward better PCI DSS, GDPR, and SOC alignment.

REFLECTION

What I learned.

A useful audit is more than a checklist. The findings have to be translated into clear priorities that reflect business impact, compliance obligations, and the organization’s ability to act.
